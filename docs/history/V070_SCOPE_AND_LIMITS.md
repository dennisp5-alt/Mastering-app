# v0.7.0 scope and limits

## Scope

Version 0.7.0 adds three offline, per-song modules to the autonomous candidate
competition: Section-Aware Mastering AI, AI-Generation Artifact Repair and
Transient Intelligence. They operate from the authoritative stereo waveform;
stems are not required and no stem waveform enters these renders.

The implementation is deterministic given the same source bytes, app version
and runtime behavior. It uses robust statistics and bounded DSP, not a cloud
model or generative waveform service.

## What “AI-Generation Artifact Repair” means

The name describes the intended repair use case. Detection is based on signal
signatures only. The app does not infer, certify or report whether a recording
was generated by AI. Fizz, metallic-band energy and phasey high-frequency side
behavior can occur in many recording and production processes.

## Hard bounds

- Source format: 48 kHz stereo WAV.
- Section map confidence: at least 0.70.
- Section automation: −0.24 to +0.12 dB; renderer cap ±0.30 dB.
- Artifact attenuation: maximum 0.55 dB combined.
- Transient attenuation: maximum 0.30 dB.
- All v0.7 edits combined: maximum 0.75 dB nominal.
- Artifact automatic coverage: maximum 12% per signature kind.
- Section automatic coverage: maximum 65%.
- Protected boundaries: first 2 seconds and final
  `max(8 seconds, 4% of duration)`.
- Source-correlation floor: 0.9955.
- Maximum LRA reduction: 0.50 LU.
- Promotion: 0.055 score margin, 0.100 targeted benefit and the relevant
  measured module-benefit gate.

The loudness goal and true-peak ceiling remain −14.00 LUFS-I and −1.00 dBTP,
respectively, subject to the established dynamics-first priority hierarchy.

## Known limits

- Learned section labels are candidates, not composer-confirmed structure.
- A short tonal signature is evidence for repair, not proof of audibility or
  source provenance.
- Centre/side and band-limited evidence cannot isolate a vocal or instrument.
- Transient classification can protect attacks conservatively; it may bypass
  useful control rather than risk damaging drums.
- Broad recurring signatures are reported and bypassed, so some systematic
  problems require mix revision or qualified human mastering.
- Response simulations cannot prove physical-device translation.
- Host/JVM tests cannot execute the phone's Android AAC codec, MediaPlayer,
  document provider or in-place update behavior.
- Final release choice still requires matched-level human listening.
- Production/A&R competitiveness is outside the app's technical verdict.
